# Method note 1: epidemics, child deaths and reused names

Written by the coordinator, 12 Nov 2026, at Kevin's request. For the Duska, Demko and Places agents. Ledger ruling X-017 makes section 3 binding.

Status: a first pass. Eight open-web searches, read at the level of abstracts and search extracts, not full papers. No surname was searched. Every dated claim below carries its source; anything marked "recalled" comes from training and can only suggest a test (X-010).

## 1. Why this matters for the tree

Roughly three in ten children born in nineteenth-century Hungary died before their first birthday. Families reused the name of a dead child for a later one. So a baptism entry with the right name and the right parents is not yet the ancestor: it may be an older sibling who died, and the ancestor may be a second child of the same name baptised years later. The same years that killed children also killed spouses, which produced fast remarriages, half-siblings and children raised in other households.

- Infant mortality: about 300 deaths per 1,000 live births in Hungary, against about 150 in Sweden, on figures published in 1882. Source: Varga, in Orvostörténeti Közlemények 202-205 (2008), https://library.hungaricana.hu/hu/view/ORSZ_ORVO_OK_2008_202_205/?pg=10
- Name reuse in Hungarian registers, stated as a working rule by a practitioner: if the baptism you found is about ten years off, keep looking, because it may be an older sibling who died in infancy. Source: https://thehungaryexchange.blogspot.com/2013/01/hungarian-genealogy-research-tip-6.html
- The same given name also recurs when a man remarries after his first wife's death. Source: Czechoslovak Genealogical Society International forum, https://cgsi.org/comment/659
- A name given to a newborn after a dead relative is called a necronym. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necronym

## 2. The bad years

### Cholera

| Wave | Notes for this region |
|---|---|
| 1831-32 | First wave. About 100,000 deaths in Hungary. Zemplén, next to Ung, was among the first counties struck. A crop failure and the epidemic set off the East Slovak Peasant Uprising of July and August 1831 in Zemplín, Abov, Šariš and Spiš: about 150 villages and 40,000 people |
| 1848-49 | Second wave, during the revolution and war |
| 1854-55 | Third wave |
| 1866-67 | Fourth wave |
| 1872-73 | Fifth wave. In one Greek Catholic parish of Zemplén (Kis Dobra), by the summer of 1873 nearly every death entry gives cholera as the cause. In Zvolen County deaths doubled |
| 1892-93 | A later wave named in Hungarian literature |

Sources: Golian, "Possibilities of studying epidemics of cholera", Studia Historiae Oeconomicae 40/1 (2022), https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/sho/article/download/37027/31806/79981 ; Golian, Historický časopis 72/3 (2024), https://historickycasopis.sk/32024/golian-jan-anti-epidemic-measures-of-the-hungarian-government-in-reaction-to-the-cholera-wave-of-1872-73.pdf ; Konyova, "Cholera Epidemic in 1831 in Zemplín County", https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/acm-2024-0018 ; first counties struck, https://epa.hu/02100/02120/00010/pdf/BPTM_TBM_10_219-234.pdf ; uprising, https://www.regionsaris.sk/en/?p=2134 ; Kis Dobra, https://kinshipgenealogy.blogspot.com/2020/04/death-and-love-in-time-of-cholera-in.html ; 1892-93, https://kozlekedesimuzeum.hu/download/BxsTded9CUl1q4xHbRk2mAbKR6L4qwq3 ; ten epidemics between 1831 and 1916, five of them large, https://demografia.hu/kiadvanyokonline/index.php/kutatasijelentesek/article/download/1955/2123

Two points for reading registers:

- From the first wave on, the state kept its own counts of the sick and the dead by county, beside the church registers. Source: https://toolkit.ecn.cz/img_upload/072068ac6406072f5d0a058e2b0b6778/hd50-1-01_web.pdf
- Church records for 1831 can be thin. A study of Lutheran congregations found the official church record gives little sight of the epidemic. Source: https://ojs.uni-eszterhazy.hu/index.php/ECEHS/article/view/1243 . So a missing burial in an epidemic year is weak evidence that someone lived.

Not yet established: which waves reached Senné, Iňačovce and Vysoká nad Uhom, and how hard. Ung is not in the list of first counties struck in 1831. The catalogued Senné Roman Catholic register begins in 1831. Assigned to Places (section 4).

### Other killers

- Diphtheria became established in Hungary in 1873. Source: https://real.mtak.hu/177142/1/650-article-p357.pdf . In Hungarian registers it appears as *torokgyík*. Source: https://community.familysearch.org/en/discussion/comment/575854
- Smallpox: Budapest had outbreaks in 1874-75 and again for four years from 1879, with only about a quarter of infants vaccinated. Cholera, smallpox, enteric fever, diphtheria, scarlet fever and measles are named together as the main epidemic killers. Source: Orvostörténeti Közlemények 78-79 (1976), https://library.hungaricana.hu/hu/view/ORSZ_ORVO_OK_1976_078_079/?pg=68
- Hunger years: the European potato failure of 1845-47, and a penury in Borsod and Zemplén in 1879-80 after cold wet years. Sources: https://amp.rte.ie/amp/1276178 ; https://hunghist.org/83-articles/622-2020-2-bodovics
- Recalled, not sourced: typhus followed the hunger years and the wars of 1848-49 and 1914-18; the influenza of 1918-19; tuberculosis throughout. Recalled terms worth checking against a glossary before use: *himlő* (smallpox), *hagymáz* (typhus or typhoid), *vörheny* (scarlet fever), *sorvadás* (wasting, often tuberculosis), *kanyaró* (measles), *vérhas* (dysentery); Latin *variolae*, *phthisis*, *dysenteria*.
- Causes of death were entered in Hungarian parish registers from the early nineteenth century. Source: Őri and Pakot, Historical Life Course Studies 16 (2026), https://hlcs.nl/article/view/25909

## 3. Rules (binding under X-017)

1. **A baptism is not an identity.** Before attaching a baptism to a person known as an adult, search the burial register of the same parish, and of the other rite's parish for a mixed couple, from the day of baptism to fifteen years after, for a child of that name and those parents. Log the search, whatever it finds.
2. **Then search forward** in the baptisms for a later child of the same name to the same father, with the same or a later wife.
3. **Two baptisms with one name and one set of parents are two people** until a record shows otherwise. Do not merge them as duplicates.
4. **If rule 1 cannot be run** because the burial register is not in hand, the link is marked `necronym risk: unresolved` and its confidence is capped at 70 (X-009 already caps it; this names the reason).
5. **Use ages.** An age at marriage, in a census or at death picks between two same-named siblings. Quote the age as written and the birth year it implies.
6. **Same name, same village, same decade is usually cousins.** Names came down from grandparents and godparents. Require both parents, and a house number where the register gives one.
7. **Epidemic years** (section 2): expect burial clusters, a parent's death, remarriage within months, half-siblings, and children living with relatives. A change of mother's name between two baptisms to one father is a second marriage until shown to be a second man.
8. **A missing burial in an epidemic year proves nothing.**
9. **Record the cause of death as written,** in the original language, in a field of its own, with your translation beside it.
